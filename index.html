— a letter —

To the one who makes the world kinder.

Elizabeth. Maame Esi. It's been eleven years. That's what I keep coming back to today — not the number itself, but what the number means.

We met in SHS. Both a little awkward, both figuring it out, and somehow — in the middle of uniforms and prep and everything else we were trying to become — we became friends. Real friends. The kind that don't expire after graduation.

Most people don't get this. Most friendships don't survive the years after school. Life pulls everybody in different directions and the group chats go quiet and eventually you just… stop. Ours didn't. Eleven years later, here we still are. Different phones, different cities sometimes, same us.

I've watched you grow up — not in the dramatic, movie-trailer way, but in the real, slow, quiet way. One day I looked up and realized the girl I used to trade notes with had become someone the whole room turns to when she walks in. You didn't lose yourself along the way. You just got more of you.

You're still the person who laughs at the exact wrong moment and somehow makes it land. Still the one who remembers things I forgot I even told you. Still the first person I text when something happens — the good, the bad, the petty, the real. Eleven years of that. I don't take it for granted.

You've been my best friend for a third of my life. I hope you know what that means.

So today is yours. Get older. Get softer. Get everything you've been quietly working toward. And if at any point this year you forget whose corner you're in — come back here, and read this line twice:

I'm still here. I've been here. I'm not going anywhere.

Happy birthday, bestie. Here's to the next eleven.
Justice
